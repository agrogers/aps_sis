## Scale for every item

- 2 = securely achieved through purposeful choices, with specific evidence of their effect across the relevant part of the piece.
- 1 = partly achieved: promising but limited, uneven, conventional, or noticeably underdeveloped.
- 0 = absent or ineffective.
- Secure does not mean flawless. An isolated slip does not cancel sustained achievement.
- Openings, developments, and endings are assessed where they occur; they need not recur throughout the piece.
- A correct account of what happened is not, by itself, evidence of effective craft. Distinguish an engaging premise from the skill with which it is written.

## Reading policy

- Read the whole piece for its intended effect before assigning item marks. Engagement may come from humour, irony, tenderness, reflection, or suspense.
- Judge literary communication, not factual plausibility or conformity to one kind of plot.
- Use ordinary reading in context. Do not invent missing events, but do not demand an explanation of every background event, motive, coincidence, or fictional mechanism.
- Distinguish a missing explanation from an actual contradiction. Identify the precise incompatible events before claiming a contradiction; a flashback followed by a return to the present is not one.
- An observer's account can vividly dramatise a scene. The narrator need not participate in the action.
- Minor surface errors belong only in the relevant language item, and only if its descriptor warrants a reduction. They do not automatically weaken register, voice, sequence, or development.
- Do not reward length, ambition, or the number of devices. Do not spread a single local weakness across unrelated items.

## AO4 (10 items)

A1 HOOK: does the opening use a distinctive perspective, question, tension, voice, or image to pull the reader on? Immediate danger is not required, but stating an ordinary problem or listing a character's circumstances is not a strong hook by itself. 1 = some interest but mainly exposition; 0 = background only.

A2 RESPONSE: does the writing build an achieved reader response across more than one passage? Emotional contrast, comic timing, or sustained irony can engage the reader without explicit suspense. 1 = intermittent effects, mainly conventional reactions, or emotion largely announced rather than created.

A3 REGISTER: is the language suited to its intended reader, form, and purpose overall? Deliberately conversational or satirical language can earn full credit.

A4 SEQUENCE: can the reader follow the central events and their connections? A change of scene or time can be brief and implicit. A story may start after background events have occurred. Require an actual obstacle to understanding, not a fuller backstory, before reducing this mark.

A5 DEVELOPMENT: do earlier details gain significance through an earned revelation, changing relationship, deepening perspective, contrast, or reversal? 2 = the development adds meaning to earlier material; 1 = a linear change, thin development, or unprepared revelation; 0 = none. Simply mentioning an object shortly before revealing its contents is not, by itself, developed preparation. A surprise twist is not required.

A6 SCENE: are key moments made vivid through well-chosen action, observation, thought, sensory detail, or speech? Dialogue and physical action are optional. Summary connecting well-realised key moments is compatible with 2. 1 = moments are only partly realised or depend mainly on stock descriptions and named feelings; 0 = mostly general reporting.

A7 PACE: does the writer control attention through changes in rhythm, focus, narrative space, scene, or reflection? Assess the whole movement of the piece, including the movement towards its climax, not just the frequency of short sentences.

A8 VOICE: does a distinctive, controlled voice or tone do work throughout? Credit sustained reflective, ironic, comic, angry, or tender perspectives. First-person narration, repeated emotional declarations, or a consistent topic do not establish a distinctive voice by themselves. 1 = intermittent personality or conventional expression; 0 = flat narration or generic preaching.

A9 DEVICES: do imagery, contrast, irony, repetition, dialogue, or other devices create specific effects in more than one passage? 2 = effective and integrated; 1 = mainly stock expressions or isolated effects; 0 = absent or ineffective. Naming a device or stating that someone is frightened is not evidence that it works.

A10 ENDING: do the final lines produce an effect earned by the preceding writing, such as an image, echo, irony, changed understanding, or resolved question? A reflective conclusion can earn 2 when the preceding events substantiate it. A surprise needs relevant preparation beyond the bare existence of a mystery. 1 = partial payoff or over-explanation; 0 = an appended lesson, an abrupt stop, or an arbitrary anticlimax that dismisses the central tension without a prepared comic or thematic payoff.

## AO5 (5 items)

B1 VOCABULARY: are choices consistently precise, varied, and purposeful, rather than merely clear or decorative? 1 = recurring generic, imprecise, or ornamental wording, or unproductive repetition. Repeated motifs, thematic words, and conversational catchphrases are not automatically imprecise, but labelling a word a motif does not make it effective.

B2 SENTENCES: is there a range of sentence structures and rhythms chosen for effect? 1 = recurring monotonous construction, not merely three similar openings. Deliberate parallelism, anaphora, fragments, and first-person narration are compatible with 2.

B3 PARAGRAPHS: does paragraphing organise the moments or ideas and guide the reader? 2 = purposeful organisation and emphasis; 1 = logical but mechanical or uneven; 0 = no useful organisation or confusing breaks.

B4 ACCURACY (meaning-based): 2 = meaning remains clear in context despite surface errors; 1 = one or two local passages require rereading or remain unclear; 0 = meaning is lost in several passages. An obvious misspelling or extra word is not, by itself, an unclear meaning.

B5 FLUENCY: do sentences sustain a readily readable flow? 2 = smooth overall; 1 = recurring constructions force rereading and interrupt the flow; 0 = many tangled or confusing sentences. Do not reintroduce spelling, punctuation, or grammar penalties here: nonstandard expression that is readily understood on first reading does not justify a reduction.
