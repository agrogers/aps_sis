# Q10 Generic Checklist Rubric (v5_generic)

Total 30 = AO4 (10 items x 2 = 20) + AO5 (5 items x 2 = 10). Strands follow the official AO4 (purpose, intended reader, form, tone, register) and AO5 (structure, vocabulary, sentence variety, paragraphing, accuracy).

## Rules
- Items are form-neutral. A story, diary, monologue, letter or article is marked with the same items.
- An item is MET only if you quote the exact words that show it. No quote means NOT MET.
- Mark each item on its own evidence. Do not let one item influence another.
- Score = number of items MET x 2 for each strand. Do not add or remove marks for any other reason.

## AO4: Purpose, reader, form, tone and register (10 items)

Purpose and reader
A1 ENGAGES: the first two sentences draw the reader in (a question, an action, a striking detail, a voice, or a specific situation).
A2 RESPONSE: at least two passages are written to produce a named reader response (e.g. suspense, amusement, unease, sympathy, surprise). Name the response in your quote.
A3 REGISTER: the language level and tone suit the intended reader throughout. Mark NOT MET if a shift in register breaks the piece (e.g. slang or formal essay language where the form does not allow it).

Form and structure
A4 SEQUENCE: beginning, development and ending each have a clear job, and each event follows from an earlier one. Mark NOT MET if a major event occurs without cause or the reader cannot follow how the situation changed.
A5 SHIFT: at least once, what the reader understands changes. This can be a reveal, a reinterpretation of an earlier event, a reversal, or a change of perspective. Quote the earlier and later passages.
A6 DRAMATISED: at least two key moments are shown in scene, with specific sensory or physical detail, rather than summarised in one line.
A7 PACE: sentence or paragraph length is varied on purpose, e.g. short lines for impact and longer ones for detail. Quote one short line used for effect.

Tone and effect
A8 TONE: one controlled tone or voice runs through at least three sections.
A9 DEVICES: at least two language devices (imagery, figurative language, repetition, contrast, irony, humour, direct address, speech) are used for effect. Quote each and state its effect in a few words.
A10 ENDING: the final lines produce an effect (an image, a line, an echo of an earlier line or image, a reversal, or an earned resolution). Mark NOT MET if the ending only summarises, states a lesson, or simply stops.

## AO5: Vocabulary, sentence structure, paragraphing and accuracy (5 items)
B1 VOCABULARY: at least three precise or varied word choices do specific work. Quote them.
B2 SENTENCES: at least three sentence types (e.g. simple, compound, complex, question, exclamation) are used, and the variety serves the piece.
B3 PARAGRAPHS: at least four paragraphs, each organising one moment or idea, with breaks that match the content.
B4 CONTROL: tense, person and punctuation are held consistently unless a change is deliberate. Quote one unintended slip if NOT MET.
B5 ACCURACY: spelling, grammar and punctuation errors do not stop the reader following the text. Minor slips do not fail this item. Quote any error that does.

## Output (one line per answer)
ID | A1..A10 (MET/NOT) | AO4 | B1..B5 (MET/NOT) | AO5 | TOTAL | one quote for the strongest MET item and one for the weakest NOT MET item
