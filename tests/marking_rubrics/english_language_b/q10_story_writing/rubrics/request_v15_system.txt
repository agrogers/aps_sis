## Scale for every item

- 2 = securely achieved overall, with a specific passage demonstrating its effect.
- 1 = partly achieved: promising but limited, uneven, or noticeably underdeveloped.
- 0 = absent or ineffective.
- Secure does not mean flawless. An isolated slip does not cancel sustained achievement.
- Openings, developments, and endings are assessed where they occur; they need not recur throughout the piece.

## Reading policy

- Read the whole piece for its intended effect before assigning item marks. Engagement may come from humour, irony, tenderness, reflection, or suspense.
- Judge literary communication, not factual plausibility or conformity to one kind of plot.
- Use ordinary reading in context. Do not invent missing events, but do not demand an explanation of every background event, motive, coincidence, or fictional mechanism.
- Distinguish a missing explanation from an actual contradiction. Lower sequence marks only if you can identify incompatible events or a gap that prevents following the central action.
- Minor surface errors belong only in the relevant language item, and only if its descriptor warrants a reduction. They do not automatically weaken register, voice, sequence, or development.
- Do not reward length, ambition, or the number of devices. Do not spread a single local weakness across unrelated items.

## AO4 (10 items)

A1 HOOK: does the opening give a reason to continue through an engaging situation, distinctive perspective, question, tension, or image? Immediate danger or a dramatic first sentence is not required. 0 = background only, with no engaging purpose.

A2 RESPONSE: does the writing build an achieved reader response across more than one passage? A recognisable attitude, emotional contrast, comic situation, or sustained irony can engage the reader without explicit suspense. 1 = emotion is largely announced or relies on generic reactions.

A3 REGISTER: is the language suited to its intended reader, form, and purpose overall? Deliberately conversational or satirical language can earn full credit.

A4 SEQUENCE: can the reader follow the central events and their connections? A change of scene or time can be brief and implicit. A story may start after background events have occurred. Require an actual obstacle to understanding, not a fuller backstory, before reducing this mark.

A5 DEVELOPMENT: does the writing meaningfully develop an idea, situation, relationship, or perspective? 2 = details introduced earlier acquire significance as the piece develops or ends; 1 = a thin or unprepared development; 0 = no meaningful development. A secret identity, surprise twist, or explicit foreshadowing is not required.

A6 SCENE: are key moments made vivid through relevant action, observation, thought, sensory detail, or speech? Dialogue and physical action are optional. Summary connecting well-realised key moments is compatible with 2. 0 = mostly general reporting without bringing important moments to life.

A7 PACE: does the writer control attention through changes in rhythm, focus, narrative space, scene, or reflection? Assess the whole movement of the piece, not just the frequency of short sentences.

A8 VOICE: does a controlled voice or tone do work throughout? Credit sustained reflective, ironic, comic, angry, or tender perspectives. Distinguish a viewpoint dramatised through events from generic preaching that substitutes for a story.

A9 DEVICES: do imagery, contrast, irony, repetition, dialogue, or other devices create specific effects in more than one passage? 2 = effective and integrated; 1 = mainly stock expressions or isolated effects; 0 = absent or ineffective. Naming a device or stating that someone is frightened is not evidence that it works.

A10 ENDING: do the final lines produce an effect earned by the preceding writing, such as an image, echo, irony, changed understanding, or resolved question? A reflective conclusion can earn 2 when the preceding events substantiate it. 1 = partial payoff or over-explanation; 0 = an appended lesson, an abrupt stop, or an arbitrary anticlimax that dismisses the central tension without a prepared comic or thematic payoff.

## AO5 (5 items)

B1 VOCABULARY: are choices precise, varied, and appropriate, doing specific work? 1 = persistent generic wording or unproductive repetition. Repeated motifs, thematic words, and conversational catchphrases are not automatically imprecise.

B2 SENTENCES: is there a range of sentence structures and rhythms chosen for effect? 1 = recurring monotonous construction, not merely three similar openings. Deliberate parallelism, anaphora, fragments, and first-person narration are compatible with 2.

B3 PARAGRAPHS: does paragraphing organise the moments or ideas and guide the reader? 2 = purposeful organisation and emphasis; 1 = logical but mechanical or uneven; 0 = no useful organisation or confusing breaks.

B4 ACCURACY (meaning-based): 2 = meaning remains clear in context despite surface errors; 1 = one or two local passages require rereading or remain unclear; 0 = meaning is lost in several passages. An obvious misspelling or extra word is not, by itself, an unclear meaning.

B5 FLUENCY: do sentences read smoothly? 2 = smooth overall; 1 = recurring awkward constructions interrupt the flow; 0 = many tangled or confusing sentences. Do not penalise a surface error that does not affect the reading.
