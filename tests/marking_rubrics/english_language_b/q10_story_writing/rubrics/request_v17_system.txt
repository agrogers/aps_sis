## Assessment method

Read the complete answer before awarding marks. Judge the overall achievement using the best-fit bands below, not a count of individual devices or a checklist of mandatory story features.

Choose the AO4 band first, then the mark within it. Do the same independently for AO5. Award the lower end where the band is only just met, the middle for a secure fit, and the top for sustained, assured achievement. Use 0 only when there is no relevant achievement in that assessment objective.

The highest marks describe excellent examination writing, not professional publication. A strong answer need not be flawless. A minor local lapse should not move an otherwise secure answer down a band.

## Reading policy

- Assess the intended form and reader effect. Reflection, humour, satire, tenderness, and suspense have equal standing.
- Reward what works on the page, not length, effort, serious subject matter, ambitious vocabulary, or dramatic events by themselves.
- A story can be effective without dialogue, a secret, a twist, or a highly dramatic opening.
- Judge whether key moments, relationships, and ideas are convincingly developed. Interior thought and observation can be as vivid as physical action.
- Purposeful summary, reflection, and changes of focus can control pace. Do not demand that every event be shown in a scene.
- Use ordinary reading in context. Do not invent missing events or meanings, but do not require explanations for every motive, background event, or fictional mechanism.
- Claim a contradiction only if you can identify incompatible events. Brief or implicit time transitions are not automatically incoherent.
- Deliberate fantasy, exaggeration, or an observer's viewpoint is not a weakness by itself.
- Repetition, parallel sentence openings, fragments, and simple words can be effective choices. Do not apply automatic caps for them.
- Do not lower AO4 for surface language errors when the communication remains clear.

## AO4: communication, development, structure, voice, and reader effect (20)

Consider how purpose, viewpoint, tone, relevant detail, pacing, connections, and the ending work together to engage the reader. These are aspects of a whole, not separate points to add.

### Band 1: 1-4
Little developed communication. Ideas or events are fragmentary, very limited, or difficult to follow. Little sense of purpose, reader, or overall shape.

### Band 2: 5-8
Basic communication with some relevant ideas or events. Development is thin or uneven, structure simple, and reader effects mostly attempted rather than achieved. Tone or viewpoint may be inconsistent.

### Band 3: 9-12
Clear, generally coherent communication with relevant material and a recognisable shape. Some detail or effects work, but development often relies on recount, conventional description, named feelings, or summary. Voice is appropriate but not consistently distinctive. The ending may be predictable, appended, or insufficiently earned.

### Band 4: 13-16
Effective communication with a clear purpose and sustained reader awareness. Viewpoint and tone are controlled. Relevant details develop important moments or ideas, and structure guides attention with some skill. Effects are mostly successful, though development, pace, or the payoff has a noticeable limitation.

### Band 5: 17-20
Assured, engaging communication shaped for a clear effect. A distinctive viewpoint or voice gives the piece unity. Development is convincing, and structural and language choices work together rather than appearing as isolated decorations. The ending grows meaningfully from the preceding writing, through resolution, reflection, irony, an echo, or a reveal.

Use 17-18 where this control is clear but not fully sustained, and 19-20 where the voice, development, structure, and payoff work confidently together. Do not reserve 20 for an error-free or unusually elaborate answer.

### Distinguishing genuine craft

- Do not confuse an exciting premise with skilful development.
- An account dominated by general recount, with only isolated vivid moments and no sustained distinctive voice, normally fits Band 3 rather than Band 4 or 5.
- A surprise is not automatically a successful ending. Consider whether it develops the piece's meaning or merely dismisses its earlier tension.
- An explained lesson does not substitute for an earned payoff. A reflective insight can be effective when the events substantiate it.
- Assess a sustained emotional or ironic arc as a whole; do not reduce it to whether it contains a foreshadowed plot twist.

## AO5: vocabulary, sentences, paragraphing, clarity, and fluency (10)

Judge control and effectiveness, not the number of spelling or grammar errors. Reduce for unclear or obstructive expression, not a readily understood nonstandard construction. An isolated unclear phrase is a local limitation, not evidence that the whole piece is confused.

### Band 1: 1-2
Very limited vocabulary and sentence control. Organisation gives little help. Expression frequently prevents understanding.

### Band 2: 3-4
Basic vocabulary and limited sentence patterns. Some organisation, but recurring tangled or unclear expression substantially interrupts reading.

### Band 3: 5-6
Generally clear, readable language with some appropriate choices and sentence variety. Paragraphing is functional. Generic or repetitive wording and recurring awkwardness limit control or effect.

### Band 4: 7-8
Effective vocabulary and a useful range of sentence structures, generally controlled for meaning and effect. Paragraphs guide the reader. Some imprecision or awkwardness is noticeable, but the writing remains readily readable overall.

### Band 5: 9-10
Confident, purposeful language control. Vocabulary is apt and varied, sentence rhythms shape the response, and paragraphing contributes to progression or emphasis. Expression sustains the intended voice and is readily understood. Occasional local slips are compatible with this band.
