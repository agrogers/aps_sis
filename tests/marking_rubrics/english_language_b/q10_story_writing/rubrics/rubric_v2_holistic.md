# Q10 Creative Writing Marking Rubric (v3_holistic)

Total 30 marks = AO4 (20: communication, structure, purpose, audience, tone, effect) + AO5 (10: vocabulary, sentence control, paragraphing, accuracy).

## Process (follow in order)
0. Holistic read. Read all answers once and write a provisional order from strongest to weakest, judged on how well the story controls the reader: engagement, shape, payoff, and a deliberate ending. Do not use spelling to set this order. Keep the order for the calibration check in step 5.
1. For each answer, read the whole piece before scoring. Do not reward length in itself.
2. Decide the AO4 LEVEL (1-5) using the descriptors. Choose the level that best describes the answer overall. A level is held when most of its features are secure, not when one strong moment appears.
3. Choose the AO4 MARK within the level's band (L1 1-4, L2 5-8, L3 9-12, L4 13-16, L5 17-20). Lower half if the features are partly secure; upper half if firm and consistent.
4. Score AO5 the same way (L1 1-2, L2 3-4, L3 5-6, L4 7-8, L5 9-10). AO5 is judged on control of vocabulary and sentences first. Errors lower the level only when they are frequent in relation to the length of the piece, or when they stop the reader following the text.
5. Calibration check. Compare your totals with the provisional order from step 0. If a piece you judged clearly stronger in the holistic read scores lower in total, check whether you over-penalised its errors or under-credited its effects, and correct the marks.
6. Output one line per answer with the levels, marks, total and one quoted strength and one quoted weakness.

## AO4 descriptors (communication and structure, 20 marks)
Ask: (a) Does the story hold the reader from start to finish, with suspense, surprise, humour or emotional pull? (b) Are events developed with detail and cause and effect, rather than summarised? (c) Are devices used for effect: pace, short sentences, withheld information, reversal or twist, irony, humour, callbacks, a deliberate ending? (d) Is the voice consistent and suited to the reader?

- L1 (1-4): Little narrative shape. Events listed or summarised. Little reader awareness. Tone unclear. Ending absent or arbitrary.
- L2 (5-8): A basic opening and ending. Events developed unevenly. Effects are generic ("it was scary"). Voice mostly consistent but uncontrolled. Some sections disconnected.
- L3 (9-12): Clear, mostly controlled structure. Relevant detail. Some effects that work (pace, imagery, dialogue). Voice suits the reader. Weak points include a rushed section, a loose ending or unexplained leaps.
- L4 (13-16): Well-controlled build with a clear shape. Effects are chosen and mostly effective, for example withheld information, a twist or reversal, comic timing, irony or a callback. Specific detail. The ending is planned and connects to the opening or theme. Minor lapses only.
- L5 (17-20): Sophisticated control throughout. Choices are precise and sustained and produce a clear reader response (surprise, tension, humour, pathos). Economical development. The ending is deliberate and resonant. Very few lapses.
  A story whose final reveal reframes the whole piece, where the set-up, misdirection and payoff are planned from the opening and the voice stays controlled and funny or tense throughout, is L5 (17-20). Use the top half of the band if the reveal is planned from the opening. Do not hold such a piece to L4 because of a minor tonal wobble in the middle.

Credit explicitly: a plot twist, a reveal that recasts earlier events, comic or ironic voice used with control, and a closing line that pays off the opening. These are AO4 crafting even when the premise is unusual or comic. Do not mark down an answer for being genre-bending or implausible unless the logic breaks the reader's engagement.

## AO5 descriptors (vocabulary and accuracy, 10 marks)
Ask: (a) Is vocabulary precise and varied, chosen for effect? (b) Are sentences varied and used for effect, including short sentences for impact? (c) Is paragraphing purposeful? (d) Are errors frequent relative to length, and do they impede meaning?

- L1 (1-2): Basic, repetitive vocabulary. Simple sentences throughout. Frequent errors in every paragraph that impede meaning.
- L2 (3-4): Some varied vocabulary and sentence types. Errors are regular and noticeable, but meaning is clear.
- L3 (5-6): Mostly accurate. Vocabulary chosen with care. Sentences varied, with some effective short sentences. Errors are occasional.
- L4 (7-8): Precise, varied vocabulary. Sentence variety controlled and used for effect. Paragraphing purposeful. Errors rare and minor, relative to length.
- L5 (9-10): Sophisticated vocabulary and syntax used for effect. Near-error-free, with any slips minor.

Error rule: the level is set by the control of vocabulary and sentences. Spelling and punctuation slips move the mark down within the level, and move the level down only when the errors are in most paragraphs and stop the reader following the text. A piece with confident, varied, controlled sentences and a few regular slips is L4 or L3, not L2. Frequent errors in every paragraph, or errors that stop the reader following the text, cap AO5 at L2.

## Calibration rules
- Length, a dramatic premise, or a high volume of events do not earn marks.
- Clean, correct but flat, summary-style narration is at most L2 for AO4.
- A reveal or twist that the ending builds to is a strong marker of L4 or L5 AO4, whatever the spelling.
- Score each answer independently in steps 1-4. Use the step 0 order only as the check in step 5.

## Output format (one line per answer)
ID | AO4 level | AO4 mark | AO5 level | AO5 mark | TOTAL | quoted strength; quoted weakness
